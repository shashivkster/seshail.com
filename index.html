Advisory

Three questions a board actually asks about technology.

Most technology conversations stall because they stay technical. Mine begin with the number that has to move, then work back to the system, the contract or the operating model that will move it.

Growing revenue

Transformation is only worth the investment when it ends in an order. I help organisations choose and stand up the platforms customers transact on, and redesign the processes behind them so demand does not leak between the click and the delivery.

  • E-commerce platform selection for a leading tyre manufacturer
  • Digital transformation for a luxury retail group
  • CRM evaluation for a pharmaceutical client
  • Advisory on a national payment system, Bhutan

Managing cost

Technology cost is decided at the contract, not the invoice. I run sourcing and vendor selection, review the agreements that lock in a decade of spend, and establish the vendor management discipline that keeps a good deal from quietly becoming a bad one.

  • Turnkey managed services contract review, South African telecom major
  • Strategic IT outsourcing for an FMCG conglomerate
  • Technology sourcing advisory for an NBFC
  • Vendor management office for a financial services firm

Reducing risk

Large programmes rarely fail suddenly; they drift. I do the diligence before the deal, the assurance during delivery and the control design after go-live, so exposure is found by the review rather than by the regulator, the acquirer or the customer.

  • IT due diligence across cement, road infrastructure and skincare
  • Segregation of duties tooling for a manufacturer
  • Technology policy drafting for a power transmission company
  • Post-implementation ERP review for a process manufacturer
Practice areas

Eight stages, from the first strategy conversation to the benefit landing.

Technology value leaks at the joints between these stages, not inside them — a strategy nobody sourced against, a system nobody reviewed after go-live, a solution live but unadopted. I work across the whole sequence, and clients engage me at whichever point they are stuck.

      Collaboration

      A good deal of the best work arrives through someone else.

      Nineteen years in professional services has taught me that the interesting mandates rarely come from a cold approach. They come from another adviser who needs depth they do not hold, or a firm that has the client relationship and wants the method. I am also glad to mentor or advise early-stage companies. I am open to all of the following.

      Independent consultants and boutiques

      Associate capacity on ERP selection, sourcing exercises, due diligence and programme assurance — under your brand or alongside it, whichever the client relationship requires.

      Systems integrators and implementation firms

      Selection, business case and PMO work that sits ahead of a build. I do not implement, so there is no competitive overlap and no argument about who owns the delivery.

      Channel and distribution partners

      Advisory-led routes to market for enterprise software, with entitlement and renewal discipline attached to the transaction rather than sold as an afterthought.

      Sector and domain specialists

      Pairing deep industry knowledge with technology method. The domain expert frames the problem; I bring the evaluation structure and the vendor market.

      Product firms entering enterprise markets

      What regulated and heavy-industry buyers actually ask for — commercial models, security posture, references and procurement readiness — from the side of the table that says no.

      Start-ups seeking a mentor or adviser

      Early-stage founders building for enterprise buyers, where the useful thing is knowing how large organisations actually evaluate, procure and adopt. Any such arrangement is subject to clearance under my current firm’s independence and conflict-of-interest policy.

      Boards, institutes and industry bodies

      Advisory board seats, guest teaching, panel contributions and joint research on digital transformation in Indian and Gulf enterprise.

      If you have a mandate that needs a second pair of hands, or a capability gap you would rather partner on than build, I would like to hear about it — including the ones that are still only an idea.
