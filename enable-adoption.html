Enable adoption

A system that is live is not a benefit. It is the precondition for one. The distance between the two is where most transformation value is lost.

Key offerings

Value realisation

BDO services may be leveraged for further helping with enablement of solution

The stage that gets cut first

Adoption work is scheduled last, funded from whatever contingency survives, and therefore reduced or removed whenever delivery runs late — which it does. The programme closes on the day the system starts, the team disperses, and the benefits in the business case become nobody's responsibility. Two years later the investment is remembered as disappointing without anyone being able to say precisely why.

The remedy is not more training. It is treating benefit as a deliverable with an owner, a measure and a date, carried past go-live rather than filed at approval.

Value leaks in predictable places

People continue using the process the system replaced. A capability that was paid for is never switched on. Data quality falls below the threshold the new reporting depends on. Local variants reappear because standardisation was agreed but never enforced. Each is measurable, and each is correctable while the programme's authority is still fresh — which is precisely when nobody is looking.

Measurement matters more than exhortation. Adoption reported as a number, by team, on a cadence, changes behaviour in a way that another communication campaign does not.

Typically called in when

  • The system is live and the benefits case has gone quiet
  • Usage is patchy and varies sharply between teams
  • Legacy spreadsheets and shadow processes are still in daily use
  • Modules that were licensed have never been switched on
  • The board is asking what the investment returned
  • A second wave of roll-out is planned and the first has not settled
  • Support volumes suggest people are working around the design
Scope

What the work covers.

Benefits baseline

The original business case reopened, its assumptions restated in current terms, and each benefit given an owner and a measure.

Adoption measurement

Actual usage by team, role and transaction type — reported as a number rather than inferred from training attendance.

Value leakage analysis

Where the promised benefit is being lost: unused capability, retained workaround, data quality, or standardisation that was agreed but not enforced.

Role-based enablement

Support built around what each role must now do differently, rather than generic system training delivered once before go-live.

Super-user networks

Local capability that persists after the programme leaves, with a defined role rather than an informal reputation.

Process compliance monitoring

Whether the redesigned process is being followed, measured in the system rather than asserted in a steering pack.

Capability activation

Licensed functionality that was never enabled — frequently the cheapest available benefit in the whole estate.

Benefits reporting

A short, honest report to the sponsor and board on what has landed, at a cadence that permits correction.

Improvement backlog

Remaining opportunities prioritised and handed to a business owner, so improvement continues after the engagement ends.

Method

How the work runs.

Usually starting three months after go-live and running for two to three quarters.

  1. Reopen the business case

    Recover what was promised, restate the assumptions in today's terms, and assign each benefit to a named owner.

  2. Measure adoption honestly

    Instrument actual usage by team and role. The variation between teams is normally larger than anyone expects and is the most useful finding.

  3. Locate the leak

    Compare intended process to observed behaviour and find where the value is escaping — and, more usefully, why.

  4. Intervene where it pays

    Enablement, configuration change, capability activation or enforcement, chosen by what the measurement shows rather than by preference.

  5. Report to the board

    A plain statement of benefit landed against benefit promised, including the parts that have not arrived.

  6. Hand to business as usual

    Owners, measures and a backlog transferred to the business, so improvement outlives the engagement.
